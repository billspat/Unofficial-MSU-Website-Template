The Deep Regulome Initiative: Foundation Models for Predicting Non-Coding Variant Pathogenicity

Developing high-capacity, multi-resolution deep neural networks trained on petabase-scale human sequence datasets to forecast cell-type specific epigenetic perturbation caused by non-coding somatic and germline mutations.

Project Overview & Motivation

Although genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have uncovered tens of thousands of loci associated with human disease, over 93% of these signals reside in non-coding regulatory sequences such as enhancers, promoters, and insulators. Deciphering the causal mechanisms behind these variants has historically been limited by cell-type specificity and experimental throughput.

The Deep Regulome Initiative establishes an end-to-end computational framework capable of predicting changes in chromatin accessibility (ATAC-seq), transcription factor occupancy (ChIP-seq), and gene expression (RNA-seq) at single-nucleotide resolution across more than 200 human tissues and cell lineages.

Specific Research Aims

Aim 1: Multi-Scale Sequence Pretraining

Years 1–2

Train a 1.2-billion parameter autoregressive and masked-language foundation model over all high-quality vertebrate reference genomes using Spartan HPC computing clusters.

Aim 2: Epigenetic Transfer & Saturation Mutagenesis

Years 2–3

Fine-tune multi-task prediction heads against ENCODE4 and 4D Nucleome experimental tracks. Perform in-silico saturation mutagenesis across 100,000 clinically uncharacterized loci.

Aim 3: Experimental CRISPR Validation

Years 3–4

Collaborate with MSU wet-lab partners to validate high-scoring non-coding variant predictions in induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) cardiac and neuronal differentiation models using prime editing.
